1. Problem Understanding & Structural Failures
Rupeezy operates one of the most attractive partner programs in the Indian brokerage industry. The Authorized Person (AP) program offers zero joining fees, 100% brokerage revenue sharing  more than double the 60ΓÇô70% industry standard  and daily payouts through the RISE Portal. Despite these compelling advantages, the program converts only 18% of incoming leads. That number is not a reflection of a weak product. It is a symptom of a fundamentally broken process.
The current model relies entirely on human Relationship Managers (RMs) to reach out to incoming partner leads, qualify them, pitch the program, handle objections, and close sign-ups. In theory, this is a reasonable approach. In practice, it breaks down in three predictable and compounding ways.
1.1 The Timing Problem
The most damaging failure in the current funnel is not human error  it is structural delay. When a lead arrives outside business hours, or during a weekend campaign, there is no mechanism to reach them. The lead simply sits in a queue. Industry research consistently shows that a prospect contacted within five minutes of expressing interest converts at nine times the rate of one contacted thirty minutes later. By Monday morning, the 50 leads from a weekend campaign are already cold.
1.2 The Language Problem
India has over 20 major languages. A financial pitch delivered in English to a Hindi-speaking MFD in Rajasthan, or a Gujarati-speaking insurance agent in Surat, is not just less effective  it is actively off-putting. The prospect disengages within the first 15 seconds. RMs typically speak one or two languages, limiting Rupeezy's addressable partner market to a fraction of its potential. Every language-mismatched call is effectively a lost lead.
1.3 The Capacity Problem
An RM handles one call at a time. A marketing campaign that generates 300 leads overnight creates a queue that backs up for days. By the time lead number 200 is called, they have forgotten they expressed interest  or they have already signed up with a competitor. There is no mechanism to surge capacity when lead volume spikes. The bottleneck is structural, not motivational.
2. Proposed Solution Overview
Our solution is an AI-powered voice agent that acts as the first line of engagement for every incoming partner lead  immediately, in their language, at any hour. The agent is not a replacement for the RM; it is a force multiplier. It handles the high-volume, time-sensitive, and language-diverse initial outreach that the RM team structurally cannot perform at scale, and it hands off only the qualified, warm, context-rich conversations to human RMs.
The agent runs a structured but adaptive sales conversation: it opens with a hook tailored to the lead's profile, pitches the key benefits of the Rupeezy AP program, handles objections naturally, qualifies interest, and routes each lead to the right next action  RM queue, WhatsApp follow-up, or long-term nurture.

3. Multilingual Conversation Handling
Language is arguably the highest-leverage variable in this system. A prospect who hears the pitch in their native language is more likely to trust the agent, engage longer, and ultimately convert. Getting this right requires more than translation it requires natural, contextual, code-mixed language behaviour that mirrors how real people in India actually speak.
3.1 Language Detection Strategy
The agent detects language at two levels. On first contact, the greeting is delivered in a neutral Hinglish opener something like 'Namaste! Main Rupeezy ki taraf se baat kar raha hoon, ek important opportunity ke baare mein' which is understood across most of the target audience and naturally prompts the lead to respond in their preferred language. That first response is the primary detection signal.
The LLM classifies the response language using script detection (Devanagari vs. Latin), vocabulary analysis, and syntactic patterns. If the response is ambiguous for example, pure numbers or very short answers the agent asks a light open-ended question to generate more signal. Detection happens within the first two exchanges.
3.2 Mid-Conversation Language Switching
Language switching is a normal, natural behaviour in Indian conversations. A lead may start in English and shift to Hindi when they get emotional or confrontational. The agent monitors every turn for language signals and switches smoothly without announcing the switch within one response. If a lead says 'Bhai, mujhe samajh nahi aaya' mid-conversation, the agent does not continue in English. It adapts immediately.
The language detection flow above illustrates how the system moves from initial input to detected language and response mode, with a mid-call switch path that activates whenever the agent detects a language change of two or more consecutive turns.
3.3 Hinglish and Code-Mixing
Hinglish is not a dialect it is the dominant communication style of urban and semi-urban India. The agent is trained with a specific Hinglish persona: short sentences, common loan words ('opportunity', 'joining fee', 'payout'), and occasional rhetorical questions in mixed language. Example: 'Aap pehle se kisi broker ke saath hain toh kya aapko 100% brokerage share aur daily payout milta hai?' This feels like a real conversation, not a translated script.
4. Objection Handling Strategy
Objections are not obstacles they are signals of engagement. A lead who raises an objection is a lead who is thinking about the offer. The agent's job is not to deflect or override; it is to listen, acknowledge, and reframe naturally, using the context of what the lead has already said.
Appendix A provides the base rebuttals for each of the five core objections. The agent ingests these as a knowledge base, not as a script. This distinction matters: the agent does not look up the objection and recite the rebuttal. It uses the rebuttal logic as a reasoning anchor and generates a response that fits the specific conversation at that moment.
The objection map above shows how each of the five core objections is handled, with the rebuttal logic derived from the knowledge base but expressed contextually. No two objection responses are identical across conversations.

4.2 Knowledge Base vs. Script Reader
The agent accesses Appendix A through a retrieval layer, not a lookup table. When an objection is raised, the relevant section is injected into the LLM context as background knowledge. The LLM then generates a response that honours the rebuttal logic while incorporating what was said earlier in the conversation  the lead's name, their current broker, their stated concerns. The result sounds like a human who listened, not a chatbot who matched a keyword.

The model runs in real time during the conversation, updating the score after each turn, and produces a final classification when the call ends.

5.4 Multi-Turn Memory
The system maintains a persistent memory store keyed by phone number and lead ID. If the same lead is contacted again either by the AI agent or by an RM the previous conversation history, score, and objections raised are loaded into context. The agent never starts from zero with a returning lead. It acknowledges the prior conversation: 'Namaste! Hum pehle baat kar chuke hain Rupeezy ke baare mein aapne kuch time maanga tha. Ab kaisa lag raha hai?'
6. Handoff Design to Human RM
The handoff is where the AI agent's value crystallizes into business outcomes. A Hot lead handed to an RM is only valuable if the RM can pick up the conversation intelligently without asking the lead to repeat themselves, without re-pitching benefits already discussed, and without losing the momentum the agent built.
6.1 Hot Lead RM Handoff
When a lead scores 75 or above, the system adds them to the RM's priority queue immediately. The RM dashboard displays a card for each Hot lead with the following information: lead name, phone number, call duration, language used, a plain-language summary of the conversation, all objections raised and how they were resolved, the lead's stated intent, and a recommended first sentence for the RM's opening call.
6.2 Warm Lead WhatsApp Fallback
For leads scoring between 45 and 74, the agent closes the call gracefully and immediately triggers an automated WhatsApp message. The message includes a personalised first line (mentioning the conversation), a brief bullet summary of the AP program benefits, and a direct sign-up link. If the lead clicks but does not complete sign-up within 48 hours, a follow-up WhatsApp is sent.

7. System Architecture
The system is built in layers, with each layer independently deployable and scalable. For the hackathon demo, the full stack can run locally with no telephony infrastructure. The architecture is designed so that each component voice interface, STT, LLM, scoring, analytics can be upgraded or replaced without rebuilding the whole system.
The architecture above shows all seven system layers, from the user-facing interface down to the data and analytics layer. The technology stack is listed on the right panel of the diagram.
7.1 Layer-by-Layer Description
Layer 1: Voice / Text Interface
For the hackathon, the interface is a browser-based voice widget using the Web Speech API for real-time transcription and playback. This demonstrates the full conversational loop without requiring Twilio or a phone number. The interface design includes a call status indicator, transcript view, and live lead score tracker visible to the demo evaluator.
Layer 2: Speech-to-Text (STT)
Deepgram is our primary STT provider for its low latency, strong Hindi support, and streaming API. Whisper (via OpenAI API) is the fallback for regional language audio where Deepgram confidence drops below threshold. For TTS, ElevenLabs provides natural-sounding Hindi and English voices; AWS Polly is the cost-efficient fallback for scale.
Layer 3: LLM Conversation Engine
The conversation engine uses GPT-4o as the primary LLM. Each turn, the agent receives: the full conversation history, the active persona and language instruction, the relevant Appendix A knowledge injected for the current conversation stage, the lead's current score, and a turn instruction (e.g., 'objection raised handle naturally'). The LLM generates the next agent turn. Claude Sonnet 4 is the fallback.
Layer 4: Knowledge Base
Appendix A is chunked and stored in a vector database (Pinecone or pgvector). At each conversation turn, the most relevant chunks are retrieved using semantic search and injected into the LLM prompt. This ensures the agent always has the right rebuttal or product fact available without hallucinating details.
Layer 5: Scoring Engine
The scoring engine runs as a lightweight Python service. It processes each conversation turn, extracts signals (via a secondary LLM call or rule-based NLP), updates the running score, and writes the score history to the database. The final classification is computed at call end.

11. Conclusion
The current 18% conversion rate is not a product problem. Rupeezy's AP program is genuinely one of the most attractive in the Indian brokerage market. The problem is structural: leads go cold before they are reached, language mismatches kill engagement within seconds, and RM capacity cannot match lead volume during campaign spikes.
The AI voice agent we have designed directly attacks all three structural failures. It contacts every lead within minutes. It speaks in their language. It runs parallel conversations without queuing. And it hands off only the qualified, context-rich conversations to human RMs making every RM hour dramatically more productive.
The scoring model ensures the right leads reach the right next step. The WhatsApp fallback keeps Warm leads warm. The post-call summaries create an institutional memory of every conversation. The dashboard gives RM leadership visibility into the full conversion funnel for the first time.
This is not an incremental improvement to the existing process. It is a structural redesign of how Rupeezy engages with its partner lead pipeline one that makes the delay, language mismatch, and capacity problems disappear by design.
